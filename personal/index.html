01 — The Spark

It started with one rusty extinguisher.

one.

Bury, March 2019. A background in architecture, a second-hand fire extinguisher nobody else saw value in, and a Facebook Marketplace listing. No plan, no investors, no company.

Fire safety is a legal duty, not a discretionary purchase. That makes it a category people buy carefully, from suppliers they trust, and one the established trade did not expect to change.

The first customer collected in person. The second wanted four hundred. The third asked whether it could be posted. Each question turned out to be the next stage of the business.

Before Freedom Fire there was one overlooked object: a rusty, second-hand extinguisher, cleaned up and listed for sale. One person took a chance on it. That single sale created the first customer, the first bit of working capital and the first glimpse that value could be found where the rest of the trade saw scrap.

In September 2019 a fire-training buyer in Bolton asked for hundreds of refurbished units for practice drills. About 400 mixed extinguishers went out for £400, roughly a pound each. Counting the time, handling and delivery it was probably a loss. It was also the first proof that the demand was real and it came in volume.

In January 2020 a takeaway in Nottingham found the listing, paid online and asked for the extinguisher to be sent. Extinguishers cannot go with any courier, so the parcel had to be packed properly and a carrier found who would take it. Once it arrived, the business was no longer limited to people who could drive to Bury.

By the summer of 2020 the operation was trading as Freedom Fire from a rented corner at the back of the family warehouse, with students cleaning, refilling and preparing hundreds of units a day, pallets going out to Scotland, Wales and London, and six containers of refurbished stock consolidated for exporters into West and Central Africa. By 2021 it was processing 800 to 1,000 units a week, and the supply of second-hand stock could no longer keep up. On 27 August 2021, Freedom Fire & Safety Ltd was incorporated.

02 — From Sale to System

Every constraint became a build.

build.

Eight stages, in order. Each one starts with a problem the market set, and ends with something that now runs on its own.

01

Second-life stock

Problem
No capital and no product. The trade scrapped serviceable extinguishers by the pallet.
Decision
Refurbish what others threw away, and sell it honestly for training and non-operational use.
Build
A cleaning, refilling and preparation bench, staffed with students. 2019 to 2021.
Result
800 to 1,000 units a week by 2021, sold to training providers, exporters and the public.
02

Ecommerce proof

Problem
Facebook Marketplace could not reach a national customer, and could not scale.
Decision
Test eBay with one listing from a personal account: a second-hand 2 kg CO2 extinguisher, December 2020.
Build
Nothing but the listing. No store, no branding, no advertising.
Result
Close to 8,000 units and more than £350,000 of sales from that one listing to date.
03

Incorporation

Problem
Sales had outgrown a sole trader, and servicing customers needed a company behind the certificate.
Decision
Incorporate, and run two engines: ecommerce and contracted servicing.
Build
Freedom Fire & Safety Ltd, 27 August 2021.
Result
Servicing for colleges, warehouses, factories and shops alongside the online range.
04

Warehouse and fulfilment

Problem
A borrowed corner cannot hold a catalogue, and marketplace buyers expect next-day delivery.
Decision
Take a first unit of our own, then move when it filled.
Build
Unit 10, about 500 square feet, April 2022. Unit 19, about three times the space, fitted out over six months and occupied from November 2024. A DHL account for next-day delivery.
Result
Thousands of orders a month fulfilled from Bury.
05

Direct manufacturing and Firestorm

Problem
After marketplace fees, packaging and delivery, stock bought through UK distributors could not compete online. Several suppliers would not extend credit to a young business.
Decision
Buy like the biggest: go direct to the manufacturer and put our own name on the product.
Build
A direct relationship agreed at the NEC in April 2024, a first order of about £90,000 funded by borrowing, the FIRESTORM mark filed with effect from 9 August 2024, and the first container landed on Christmas Eve 2024.
Result
An owned brand, registered in classes 9 and 20, on every cylinder that followed.
06

Multiple channels

Problem
One marketplace is one point of failure.
Decision
Rebuild the same catalogue, compliance and fulfilment on each new channel in turn.
Build
eBay business store, January 2024. Temu UK's first dedicated fire extinguisher seller, 2025. Amazon, OnBuy and an own Shopify store in launch through summer 2026.
Result
More than £160,000 on Temu in its first three months, with fewer than ten live products.
07

Service and B2B

Problem
Marketplace revenue is lumpy; compliance work is recurring.
Decision
Keep the vans on the road while the store grows.
Build
Servicing, installation and contracted site work for commercial premises, nationwide from Bury.
Result
About £100,000 of servicing and site work in the first full year of scaled trading, growing alongside ecommerce.
08

Systems and automation

Problem
Thousands of SKUs and orders cannot be run from a spreadsheet by one person.
Decision
Build the tools so that other people can run the business.
Build
Order management and a support desk across channels, listing and pricing workflows, and a supply-chain tracker built in-house that follows every consignment from factory to the Bury warehouse.
Result
A team, not a founder, runs the day. That is the platform the next chapters stand on.

04 — How I Build

Five decisions, and what they cost.

decide.

The same shape every time: a constraint the market set, a decision, what was actually built, the measurable result, and where the evidence sits.

01Rethinking a traditional category
Constraint
Fire safety was sold by distributors to engineers, on account, with catalogues. Nobody was selling it well to a person with a phone and a problem.
Decision
Treat the category like any other online essential: own the product, explain it plainly, ship it next day, and build the tools that answer the question before the customer has to ask.
Implementation
Second-life stock (2019), the first eBay listing (2020), a business store built over twelve months before it opened (2023), direct manufacturing and the Firestorm brand (2024), and digital buying that replaces a phone call: clear specifications, kits and delivery you can track.
Result
First of 1,193 sellers in the category on eBay UK by July 2026. Three registered brands. Temu's first dedicated fire extinguisher seller.
Evidence
YF-CH-01, YF-IP-01, YF-PR-01, YF-GR-04.
02Turning traction into influence
Constraint
A young company from Bury had no standing with manufacturers, marketplaces or the trade.
Decision
Let the numbers speak first, then use every invitation to tell the story of how they were made.
Implementation
eBay sales of £143,000 in the store's first five months, then more than £500,000 in year one and about £890,000 in year two. Named runner-up in the eBay UK Business Awards 2024 and invited to Belfast to meet eBay's UK leadership. Approached by Temu UK to open its fire extinguisher category and worked with its UK and China compliance teams to set it up around compliant, UK-held stock. A direct manufacturing partner who chose a small Bury warehouse as its first UK client.
Result
Growth over two comparable years, external recognition on the record, and a seat in the conversations that decide how the category is sold.
Evidence
YF-GR-01, YF-GR-02, YF-GR-05, YF-RC-01, YF-OP-04.
03Designing the operating system
Constraint
Every new channel and brand multiplied the work. Done by hand, the founder becomes the bottleneck.
Decision
Build one set of shared infrastructure that every product, channel and person plugs into, then hire and train people to run it.
Implementation
Direct sourcing with a supply-chain tracker built in-house. One warehouse and fulfilment operation serving every channel. Order management and a shared support desk. Listing, pricing and compliance workflows that were built once for eBay and reused for Temu, then Amazon, OnBuy and Shopify. Brand and IP held in the company's name. People brought in through placements and trained on the systems.
Result
The same category rebuilt on a second marketplace produced more than £160,000 in three months with fewer than ten products. Three more channels were prepared at the same time in 2026 without a second warehouse.
Evidence
YF-GR-04, YF-OP-02, YF-CH-02, YF-PE-02.
04Adapting under pressure

Three cases, each with a date, the decision, and what it produced.

Supplier rejection
2023 to 2024. Manufacturers did not believe extinguishers belonged online and would not extend credit; distributor pricing left no margin after marketplace fees. Decision: go direct. A manufacturing relationship agreed at the NEC in April 2024, a first order of about £90,000 met by borrowing and trading cash, the first container on 24 December 2024. Result: an owned brand, transformative unit costs, and availability that no longer depended on a distributor's stock list.
Platform dependence
2025 to 2026. More than nine tenths of online sales sat on one marketplace. Decision: rebuild the model on a second channel before it was needed, then a third and fourth. Temu went live in 2025 and returned more than £160,000 in three months; Amazon, OnBuy and an own store followed in 2026. Result: the second full year passed £1 million with growth no longer resting on a single listing or platform.
Capital and space
2023 to November 2024. £17,000 of savings and £5,000 borrowed from family went almost entirely into stock for a store that had not yet sold anything. Unit 10 filled within two years. Decision: staged scale, reinvesting trading cash, taking the unit opposite when it came free, and fitting it out over six months while still shipping. Result: about three times the floor space, thousands of orders a month, and adjacent categories such as batteries brought in on the back of it.
Evidence
YF-OP-04, YF-OP-03, YF-GR-04, YF-GR-05, YF-FN-01, YF-OP-02.
05Listening at scale
Constraint
Tens of thousands of customers, most of whom will never phone. The signal arrives as ratings, support cases, returns and the questions people ask before they buy.
The loop
Marketplace ratings and review counts by period; support cases, response and resolution times in the shared desk; refund, return, damage and defect rates; dispatch and order accuracy from the order system and carrier records. No satisfaction score is quoted here because none was formally collected before 2026; a baseline for those measures began this year and will be reported once it has a full period behind it.
Feedback that changed something
January 2020. A remote customer asked for delivery; the answer was proper packaging and a carrier who would take extinguishers, which became the fulfilment model. 2024. Smoke-alarm customers kept asking for the right batteries; batteries became a category and brand relationships followed. 2025. Temu's compliance teams needed a compliant, UK-held range with fast domestic delivery; the range was built to that specification and took the top two places in its ranking.
Result
Each change is dated, each has a measurable outcome, and the loop now runs from a shared desk rather than the founder's inbox.
Evidence
YF-OR-03, YF-OP-02, YF-GR-04, YF-RC-02.
Find the constraint, design a response, prove it in the market, then turn what you learned into something other people can run.
Tom Letcher, founder
05 — Built With People

A founder who hires is not the same as one who delegates.

people.

The roles that exist now, how responsibility was handed over, and what was built so that other people could run the work without asking.

Placements have been hosted since 2021, beginning with learners from Newbridge College, including young people with additional needs. One of the earliest placements progressed into long-term, full-time employment.

In September 2025 the company began formal T Level industry placements with support from the Growth Company. By June 2026 all three students had been offered long-term roles.

The fuller story of how people come into the company is told on The Way In. It is not repeated here.

Roles created. Web development, warehouse and fulfilment, IT, ecommerce operations and servicing, most of them filled by people who came in on a placement, through the Job Centre, or while still studying, and trained on the job.

Responsibility delegated. The website and storefronts are designed and run by the web team. Fulfilment runs from the warehouse team's own order queue. The Amazon launch was handed to a specialist agency in May 2026 rather than absorbed by the founder. Suppliers, carriers and marketplace compliance each have a named owner.

Systems built for others to use. The order system, the shared support desk, the listing workflows and the supply-chain tracker exist so that a new starter can do useful work in their first week, and so that nothing depends on one person remembering.

I thought the placement would be a basic storefront. A few product pages, prices on, done. Instead I was given creative freedom. Told what the business needed, then trusted to work out how it should look and work. It turned into a proper, highly designed website. Not many places would hand that much responsibility to someone on a placement, and when I got things wrong, they showed me why and let me fix it.
Bill, web developer · joined on a T Level placement, September 2025

08 — Still Building

The line does not stop here.

The next stage is not a forecast. It is the same method applied again: find the constraint, design the response, prove it in the market, and turn it into something other people can run. The trading company has the brands, the sourcing, the warehouse, the systems and the people. What is being built now is the second and third channel at full scale, the trade side of the business, and the next owned brands. Everything beyond that is an option the model keeps open, and it will be described as an option until the register says otherwise.

05 · Training room

Know your fire

Every starter learns this in week one. Pick what's burning — we'll show you what puts it out. Or test yourself: six callouts, scored.

0 / 6

Water

Wood, paper, textiles. Never near live electrics.

Foam

Solids and flammable liquids — smothers the surface.

CO₂

Liquids and live electrical — leaves no residue.

Dry powder

A, B, C and electrical — not in enclosed spaces.

Wet chemical

Cooking oils and fats — the chip-pan specialist.

Class A — wood, paper, textiles. Water, foam, powder or wet chemical will do it.

07 · The code

Four things we don't bend on

01

Certified, or not at all

If it cannot be certified and documented, it does not get signed off. The paperwork is the product as much as the hardware is.

02

Say the real date

Because we track our own stock from origin, we can give a delivery date we actually believe — and say so plainly when it slips.

03

Explain it properly

Most clients did not choose to become the responsible person. Part of the job is making the duty understandable rather than frightening.

04

Keep the door open

Hire on attitude, fund the tickets, mentor on the vans — so the trade has engineers in ten years' time. Section 04 is how.

01 — The Spark

It started with one rusty extinguisher.

one.

Bury, March 2019. An architecture background, a second-hand extinguisher nobody else saw value in, and a Facebook Marketplace listing.

The first customer collected in person. The second wanted four hundred. The third asked whether it could be posted. Each question became the next stage of the business.

Before Freedom Fire there was one overlooked object: a rusty, second-hand extinguisher, cleaned up and listed for sale. One person took a chance on it. That sale paid for the next, and what the trade called scrap turned out to be a market.

A Bolton training buyer took 400 units for £400. A Nottingham takeaway paid online and asked for delivery. By 2021 a corner of the family warehouse was turning out 800 to 1,000 units a week, and on 27 August 2021 Freedom Fire & Safety Ltd was incorporated.

The full origin

September 2019. About 400 mixed extinguishers to a fire-training buyer in Bolton for £400, roughly a pound each and probably a loss once time and delivery were counted. The first proof that demand was real and came in volume.

January 2020. A Nottingham takeaway paid online and asked for the extinguisher to be sent. Extinguishers cannot go with any courier, so the parcel had to be packed properly and a carrier found. From then on the business was not limited to people who could drive to Bury.

Summer 2020. Trading as Freedom Fire from a rented corner of the family warehouse: students cleaning and refilling hundreds of units a day, pallets to Scotland, Wales and London, and six containers of refurbished stock for exporters into West and Central Africa. By 2021 the supply of second-hand stock could no longer keep up.

02 — From Sale to System

Every constraint became a build.

build.

Eight stages, in order. Each starts with a problem the market set, shows what was built in answer, and ends with the result that now runs on its own.

01

Second-life stock

Problem
No capital and no product. The trade scrapped serviceable extinguishers by the pallet.
Build
A refurbishing bench, staffed by students, selling honestly for training and non-operational use. 2019 to 2021.
Result
800 to 1,000 units a week by 2021, sold to training providers, exporters and the public.
02

Ecommerce proof

Problem
Facebook Marketplace could not reach a national customer, and could not scale.
Build
One eBay listing from a personal account in December 2020: a second-hand 2 kg CO2 extinguisher. No store, no branding, no advertising.
Result
Close to 8,000 units and more than £350,000 of sales from that one listing to date.
03

Incorporation

Problem
Sales had outgrown a sole trader, and servicing customers needed a company behind the certificate.
Build
Freedom Fire & Safety Ltd, 27 August 2021, running two engines: ecommerce and contracted servicing.
Result
Servicing for colleges, warehouses, factories and shops alongside the online range.
04

Warehouse and fulfilment

Problem
A borrowed corner cannot hold a catalogue, and marketplace buyers expect next-day delivery.
Build
Unit 10, about 500 square feet, in April 2022. Unit 19, three times the space, from November 2024. A DHL account for next-day delivery.
Result
Thousands of orders a month fulfilled from Bury.
05

Direct manufacturing

Problem
Distributor stock could not compete online after fees, packaging and delivery, and suppliers would not extend credit.
Build
A direct manufacturer relationship agreed at the NEC in April 2024, a first order of about £90,000 funded by borrowing, the FIRESTORM mark filed in August 2024, and the first container on Christmas Eve 2024.
Result
An owned brand on every cylinder since, and unit costs that changed the model.
06

Multiple channels

Problem
One marketplace is one point of failure.
Build
The same catalogue, compliance and fulfilment rebuilt on each channel in turn: eBay business store in January 2024, Temu in 2025, with Amazon, OnBuy and an own Shopify store being prepared for late 2026.
Result
More than £160,000 on Temu in its first three months, with fewer than ten live products.
07

Service and B2B

Problem
Marketplace revenue is lumpy; compliance work is recurring.
Build
Servicing, installation and contracted site work for commercial premises, nationwide from Bury.
Result
About £100,000 of servicing and site work in the first full year of scaled trading.
08

Systems and automation

Problem
Thousands of SKUs and orders cannot be run from a spreadsheet by one person.
Build
Order management, a support desk across channels, listing and pricing workflows, and an in-house supply-chain tracker that follows every consignment from factory to Bury.
Result
A team, not a founder, runs the day. That is the platform the next chapters stand on.

04 — How I Build

Five decisions, and what they cost.

decide.

The same shape every time: a constraint, a decision, what was built, the result, and what it changed.

01Rethinking a traditional category
Constraint
Fire safety was sold by distributors to engineers, on account, with catalogues. Nobody was selling it well to a person with a phone and a problem.
Decision
Treat the category like any other online essential: own the product, explain it plainly, ship it next day, and build the tools that answer the question before the customer has to ask.
Implementation
Second-life stock (2019), the first eBay listing (2020), a business store built over twelve months (2023), direct manufacturing and the Firestorm brand (2024), and buying that replaces a phone call: clear specifications, kits and tracked delivery.
Result
First of 1,193 sellers in the category on eBay UK by July 2026. Three registered brands. Temu's first dedicated fire extinguisher seller.
02Turning traction into influence
Constraint
A young company from Bury had no standing with manufacturers, marketplaces or the trade.
Decision
Let the numbers speak first, then use every invitation to tell the story of how they were made.
Implementation
eBay sales of £143,000 in the store's first five months, more than £500,000 in year one and about £890,000 in year two. Runner-up in the eBay UK Business Awards 2024, with an invitation to Belfast to meet eBay's UK leadership. Approached by Temu UK to open its fire extinguisher category. A direct manufacturing partner that chose a small Bury warehouse as its first UK client.
Result
Growth over two comparable years, recognition, and a seat in the conversations that decide how the category is sold.
03Designing the operating system
Constraint
Every new channel and brand multiplied the work. Done by hand, the founder becomes the bottleneck.
Decision
Build one set of shared infrastructure that every product, channel and person plugs into, then hire and train people to run it.
Implementation
Direct sourcing with an in-house supply-chain tracker. One warehouse serving every channel. Order management and a shared support desk. Listing, pricing and compliance workflows built once for eBay and reused for Temu, and now being reused for Amazon, OnBuy and Shopify. Brand and IP held in the company's name. People brought in through placements and trained on the systems.
Result
The same category rebuilt on a second marketplace produced more than £160,000 in three months with fewer than ten products. Three more channels were prepared in 2026 without a second warehouse.
04Adapting under pressure

Three cases, each with a date, the decision, and what it produced.

Supplier rejection
2023 to 2024. Manufacturers did not believe extinguishers belonged online and would not extend credit; distributor pricing left no margin after fees. Decision: go direct. A relationship agreed at the NEC in April 2024, a first order of about £90,000 met by borrowing and trading cash, the first container on 24 December 2024. Result: an owned brand, and unit costs that changed the model.
Platform dependence
2025 to 2026. More than nine tenths of online sales sat on one marketplace. Decision: rebuild the model on a second channel before it was needed, then a third and fourth. Temu returned more than £160,000 in three months; Amazon, OnBuy and an own store are being prepared for late 2026. Result: the second full year passed £1 million without resting on one platform.
Capital and space
2023 to November 2024. £17,000 of savings and £5,000 from family went almost entirely into stock for a store that had not yet sold anything. Unit 10 filled within two years. Decision: staged scale, reinvesting trading cash and fitting out the unit opposite over six months while still shipping. Result: three times the floor space and thousands of orders a month.
05Listening at scale
Constraint
Tens of thousands of customers, most of whom will never phone. The signal arrives as ratings, support cases, returns and pre-sale questions.
The loop
Ratings and review counts by period; support cases and resolution times in the shared desk; refund, return and defect rates; dispatch accuracy from the order system and carrier records.
Feedback that changed something
January 2020. A remote customer asked for delivery; packaging and a willing carrier became the fulfilment model. 2024. Smoke-alarm customers kept asking for the right batteries; batteries became a category. 2025. Temu's compliance teams needed a compliant, UK-held range with fast delivery; the range was built to that specification and took the top two places in its ranking.
Result
Each change is dated and measurable, and the loop runs from a shared desk, not the founder's inbox.
Find the constraint, design a response, prove it in the market, then turn what you learned into something other people can run.
Tom Letcher, founder
05 — Built With People

A founder who hires is not the same as one who delegates.

people.

The roles that exist now, how responsibility was handed over, and what was built so other people can run the work.

Placements have been hosted since 2021. Formal T Level industry placements began in September 2025; by June 2026 all three students had been offered long-term roles.

Placements

The first learners came from Newbridge College, including young people with additional needs. One of the earliest placements progressed into long-term, full-time employment. The T Level placements run with support from the Growth Company.

How people come into the company is told on The Way In.

Roles created. Web development, warehouse and fulfilment, IT, ecommerce operations and servicing, most filled by people who came in on a placement, through the Job Centre or while still studying.

Responsibility delegated. The website and storefronts are run by the web team. Fulfilment runs from the warehouse team's own order queue. The Amazon launch went to a specialist agency in May 2026. Suppliers, carriers and marketplace compliance each have a named owner.

Systems built for others. The order system, the support desk, the listing workflows and the supply-chain tracker exist so a new starter can do useful work in their first week, and nothing depends on one person remembering.

I thought the placement would be a basic storefront. A few product pages, prices on, done. Instead I was given creative freedom. Told what the business needed, then trusted to work out how it should look and work. It turned into a proper, highly designed website. Not many places would hand that much responsibility to someone on a placement, and when I got things wrong, they showed me why and let me fix it.
Bill, web developer · joined on a T Level placement, September 2025

Carbon footprint

Three places the footprint is real, and what is done about each.

Packaging

Plastic replaced by cardboard across the ecommerce operation: cartons, inserts and void fill that recycle at the kerbside. Cylinders travel in board, not foam or film.

End of life

Retired extinguishers, and any hazardous contents, go to a regulated waste transfer facility under the duty of care, with the transfer documentation kept.

Supply chain

Made in China, shipped by sea in full containers, held once in Bury and dispatched nationally. That route is where most of the footprint sits, and the first thing the baseline measures.

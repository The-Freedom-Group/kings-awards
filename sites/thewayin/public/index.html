We're Freedom Fire, a fire safety business in Bury. Hiring by CV kept missing people who could do the work.

The way in has never been one route. The first were university students at the back of a rundown warehouse. Then came supported placements from New Bridge College in 2021, then Jobcentre referrals and direct, inclusive recruitment, and since October 2025 the T Level industry placements with Bury College. Every route ends in the same place: a real role in the warehouse, on the web team, in ecommerce or in IT, a named person to learn from, and a long-term job where the work justifies it. We assess attitude, reliability, interests and potential, not a polished CV.

03The timeline

Seven years, told through the people.

The company's story from the Freedom Group timeline, kept to the parts about the people who built it: who joined, where they worked, and what they were trusted with.

  1. March 2019One person, one listing. Tom Letcher sells one refurbished extinguisher from Bury through a Facebook Marketplace listing. No team, no plan, no company.
  2. June 2020The first students. Trading as Freedom Fire from a corner of the family warehouse. University and college students join to clean, refill and prepare hundreds of refurbished units a day, with pallets going out nationwide.
  3. Aug 2021The company. Freedom Fire & Safety Ltd incorporated. Engineers now service and install extinguishers on commercial sites alongside the online range.
  4. 2021The first placements. Learners from Newbridge College, including young people with additional needs, take placements in the business. One of the earliest, Jojo, goes on to full-time work.
  5. April 2022A front door of its own. Unit 10: the first premises the company could call its own, and a base for the team.
  6. 2023The year of building. Thousands of products researched, photographed and written up by hand before the store opened. The founder's own savings fund the stock.
  7. May 2024The eBay Social Impact Award. The eBay business store opens. Freedom Fire is named runner-up in the eBay UK Business Awards for the category of social impact, and the trip to Belfast tells the story of the young people given a way in.
  8. Nov 2024Unit 19. An empty industrial shell turned into the headquarters over six months, with offices, a kitchen, fulfilment rooms and packing stations, fitted out by the team while orders kept shipping.
  9. Dec 2024The team unloading one of their containers at Christmas. Christmas Eve 2024: the first container of Firestorm fire extinguishers, the company's own brand. What a way to celebrate: a Christmas present for the whole team.
  10. Oct 2025T Level placements begin. The first T Level industry placements start with Bury College. Bill, Harrison and Gabriel join, each with a named person to learn from.
  11. Dec 2025Bill wins the Freedom Group project. At the weekly meeting Bill presents the Freedom Group project and wins it: the company's new website and storefronts, built from scratch by two T Level students.
  12. June 2026Three of three. All three of the first cohort complete their placements and move into paid summer internships, then continuing roles. The whole team marks it with a day at the races.
  13. Aug 2026A million-pound year. Combined sales pass £1 million. Containers arrive back to back, the company is number one for extinguishers on eBay and Temu, and launches on Amazon and Shopify are in preparation.
  14. Sept 2026The King's Awards for Enterprise. The company enters the King's Awards for Enterprise under the Promoting Opportunity category, with The Way In as the company's initiative and the founder's portfolio alongside it.
  15. Oct 2026The second cohort, and the Jobs Guarantee. Three more Bury College T Level students will arrive, and two young people will be recruited through the Government's Jobs Guarantee, part of the Youth Guarantee, with the Growth Company. The newest ways in.

05The impact

Four groups. What changed.

What changed for the students, the business, the staff and the town.

01

The people supported

Nine people supported in 2026, seven of them from disadvantaged backgrounds. Seven in paid work. All three Bury College T Level students progressed into paid roles.

02

The organisation

Roles filled across warehouse, IT, web and ecommerce. The website, quoted at about £40,000 by an agency, was built over six months by two T Level students who arrived on placements.

03

The employees

Experienced staff mentor every starter. At the weekly meeting a different colleague presents their work and takes feedback from the team. Placement students now mentor the next intake.

04

The wider community

Two talks and a workshop at New Bridge College, recruitment fairs, and a voice in how the Jobs Guarantee is run. Families see confidence return: one mother wrote that she had got her son back.

07How we learn

Feedback, then a change.

What people told us, and what changed because of it. Feedback comes from daily one-to-ones, reviews, the weekly meeting, the participant films, Bury College's T Level workbooks, and from tutors and families.

The participants
Said
Real responsibility makes them feel trusted, and regular guidance makes unfamiliar work less intimidating. Some found unclear tasks overwhelming, or were nervous speaking in front of others.
Changed
Clearer written job lists, projects split into achievable stages, tasks matched to strengths, and responsibility increased gradually.
Followed
At the weekly meeting a different colleague now presents their work and takes feedback from the room, so confidence is built by everyone rather than handed down by the founder.
Circumstances
Said
Confidence, communication and routine were bigger barriers than technical ability. Some needed flexibility around education, travel or home life.
Changed
Hours adjusted around courses, equipment provided, one-to-one support increased where needed, and adjustments offered before anyone starts.
Followed
People a standard interview would have missed are in long-term roles. Jojo has five years without a sick day.
Families and tutors
Said
Josh's mum wrote unprompted that “all hope was lost” after the family's losses, and then: “I can finally say ‘I got my son back’.” Tutors and families give the independent view of change.
Changed
No satisfaction ratings are claimed until they are measured. Anonymous feedback at the start, midpoint and end of every placement is being formalised, alongside a central participant register.
Followed
The first full year of measured feedback is reported here when it is complete.
